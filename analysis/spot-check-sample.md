## Synopses to check (random new books)

1. **Gesaku Zanmai** — Ryūnosuke Akutagawa, 1917 [FICTION] (/ryunosuke-akutagawa/gesaku-zanmai)  
   One autumn day in 1831, the aging writer Bakin endures a bathhouse critic's sneers, a pushy publisher and his own flat prose, then lifts himself out of despair by writing through the night. A portrait of an artist's pride, doubt and absorption in his work.

2. **Dogra Magra** — Yumeno Kyūsaku, 1935 [CRIME & MYSTERY] (/yumeno-kyusaku/dogra-magra)  
   A man wakes in a cell of a Kyushu university asylum with no memory of who he is, while two rival professors feed him a cursed manuscript, a ghastly murder case and a theory of ancestral dreams. Each document overturns the last in a looping, maddening labyrinth of mind and heredity.

3. **Farewell** — Osamu Dazai, 1945 [FICTION] (/osamu-dazai/farewell)  
   A retired country doctor, pressed by a newspaper man hunting for a heartwarming Sino-Japanese story, recalls his Sendai classmate Zhou Shuren, the future Lu Xun. Through nights at an inn in Matsushima and talk of science, literature and China, he pieces together the young Zhou's idealism and the day he abruptly left medical school.

4. **The Spider's Thread** — Ryūnosuke Akutagawa, 1918 [FICTION] (/ryunosuke-akutagawa/the-spider-s-thread)  
   Buddha, strolling beside the lotus pond of Paradise, spots the thief Kandata suffering in the Blood Pond of Hell and lowers a single silver spider thread to him for the one kindness of his life. Kandata climbs, but when he kicks away the other sinners following him, the thread snaps.

5. **Home Life in Tokyo** — Jukichi Inouye, 1910 [ESSAYS & JOURNALISM] (/jukichi-inouye/home-life-in-tokyo)  
   A Japanese insider walks English readers through the Tokyo household: food and sake, kimono and hair-styling, the house and its servants, marriage, childbirth, funerals, and festivals. Chapters on music, theatre, flower-arranging and parlour games like sugoroku and ode-cards round out a year of ordinary life.

6. **The Fatalist** — Kunikida Doppo, 1903 [FICTION] (/kunikida-doppo/the-fatalist)  
   On a winter beach at Kamakura, a narrator reading in the dunes meets a haggard stranger who digs up a hidden bottle of brandy. The man tells him his life: raised as another man's son, he marries Satoko and learns that his adoptive mother is his true mother and his wife is his half-sister, proof to him that fate cannot be escaped.

7. **Rush to Accuse** — Osamu Dazai, 1940 [FICTION] (/osamu-dazai/rush-to-accuse)  
   Judas Iscariot bursts in to inform on his master, pouring out jealousy, love and contempt for the man he follows. His rambling denunciation shows that his betrayal is rooted in anguished devotion.

8. **Out of the East: Reveries and Studies in New Japan** — Lafcadio Hearn, 1895 [ESSAYS & JOURNALISM] (/lafcadio-hearn/out-of-the-east-reveries-and-studies-in-new-japan)  
   Hearn dreams his way through Japan in eleven sketches, from the fisher-boy Urashima's vanished centuries to a hotel balcony by the sea. He quizzes Kyushu students on Western stories, watches jiujutsu masters throw men with a touch, and follows soldiers marching to the Chino-Japanese war. He keeps asking what the Japanese mind sees that the West cannot.
